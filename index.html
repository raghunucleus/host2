04 What real engineering takes

Not a prompt monkey. A real engineer who happens to be AI-native.

Four pillars an ALP graduate is trained on. Each one is something a vibe coder can't fake under interview pressure — and exactly what a hiring manager screens for.

A vibe coder ships a demo. An ALP engineer ships a system.

There's a generation of self-taught coders right now who can prompt a chatbot into producing something that runs. That isn't engineering. Engineering is knowing why it runs, what it'll do at scale, where it'll break, and what business problem it actually solves. ALP grads are trained on all four — and can prove it on the parts that matter.

Named tools used daily. Claude, Cursor, Antigravity, GitHub Copilot. Prompt engineering, context engineering, agent design. Fluency, not novelty.

◈ Domain Depth

▦ System Design

Before code, the diagram. Scaling, caching, queues, data models, trade-offs. The interview round that breaks self-taught coders is the one ALP grads ace.

06 Why it actually works

Three design choices. Everything else is a consequence.

The reason ALP works isn't a syllabus. It's these three refusals — the things we won't compromise on, no matter the batch.

REFUSAL 01

One ladder. Climbed in public.

Skill is measured against a single live coding ladder that companies recognise. Where you sit on it decides what's open to you — and the ladder is real, not self-reported.

REFUSAL 02

You train. You don't just listen.

Most of your day isn't lecture. It's reps — solving, designing, building, shipping — alongside coaches who've already climbed the ladder you're on.

REFUSAL 03

Real work, inside real companies.

The last stretch isn't a simulation. You sit inside a product team, ship code that ships to users, get reviewed by working engineers. That's what the resume actually proves.
